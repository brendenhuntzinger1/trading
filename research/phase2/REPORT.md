# Phase 2 report: strategy discovery (2026-10-26)

**Bottom line.**
- Seven economically distinct families were implemented and tested on development data, 37 configurations
  in all.
- **None qualified.** Every family's validation (2023-01 → 2024-06) and final (2024-07 → 2026-10-23) windows
  are therefore still unread.
- Two families moved the evidence: an ML ranking signal that is real but small (F6/F7), and the finding that
  **execution timing**, not prediction, dominated costs.
- Nothing here is a profitable strategy, and nothing is traded.

All numbers come from `research/phase2/results/*.json`. The table (`results/TABLE.md`) is generated by
`make_table.py`. Criteria are in `PHASE2_PROTOCOL.md` (plus Amendments 1–2, all made before the results they
govern). The trial count is cumulative: 145 including the 108 earlier ones.

## 4. What each result taught us
1. **Costs depend on *when* you trade more than on *what* you trade.**
   - Measured quoted half-spreads (3,840 SIP quotes): 5–26 bp at 09:31 against 1.4–3.7 bp at 15:30–15:59.
   - The same ML signal lost about 12 bp/day open-to-open and made +5 to +7 bp/day close-to-close.
2. **Classic published intraday effects did not show up in 2020–22 (or 2016–22 for ETFs)** for intraday
   momentum, end-of-day reversal and short-term reversal. This matches the decay literature. Ideas from
   1990s–2010s papers need fresh evidence, not trust.
3. **ML adds a little, consistently.** The boosted-tree ranker beat linear reversal every year, and its signal
   grew in 2022. Its weakness is dispersion and concentration, not absence.
4. **Retail patterns** (RSI(2) dips, failed breakouts) mostly collect the market's own drift, or a bounce
   smaller than the spread.
5. **Process:** two look-ahead traps were caught: incident 20 (in Phase 1), and incident 22 (a biased control)
   here. Also one spec flaw (F4-C2 identical to B0) and one implementation deviation (F6-C4's cost filter).
   Each is recorded.

## 5. Capabilities that improved
- **Data:**
  - consolidated daily panel for the whole US universe, 2016 → 2026-10-23 (19,878 symbols);
  - minute bars for 13 ETFs;
  - earnings-event feed with surprise verdicts (12,208 events, 2020–22);
  - measured spread model by liquidity band and minute.
- **Research machinery:**
  - protocol and stage gate enforced in code: validation and final windows can't be read without a frozen,
    qualified configuration;
  - trial ledger (`python research/ledger.py trials`) and duplicate check (`ledger.py dedup`);
  - forward-evidence recorder with staleness blocks;
  - every result table generated from result files.
- **Models:** walk-forward ML with leakage tests; cost-aware abstention that works (F6-C4 correctly chose not
  to trade).

## 6. Exact next step
**Recommendation:** spend the 2023–2024 validation window on **one** pre-registered combined candidate, not on
more development mining.
- 2020–22 has now been read by 37 configurations; more searching there mostly finds noise.
- The candidate: the F7 boosted-tree ranker traded at the close (C4: two-day hold, half the book rebalanced
  daily), with the event features from F2 added.
- Because no configuration passed development, the protocol does not allow this today. It needs either:
  - **(a)** an owner decision to run validation on the best non-qualifying configuration, labelled
    "exploratory validation" (its pass would count only as a lead requiring prospective evidence); or
  - **(b)** prospective paper trading of F7-C4 from now on. This needs the rotated Alpaca key stored as an
    environment secret, so a scheduled job can read data and log predictions before outcomes.

**(b) is the cleaner evidence** and also covers the "current market" requirement. It costs nothing but
calendar time: three months gives about 60 sessions.
