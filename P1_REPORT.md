# P1 report: cross-sectional crypto return prediction

Run 2026-10-26 under `P1_PREREGISTRATION.md`, original text plus Amendment 1, both registered before any model
was fit. Code: `research/p1/study.py`; results: `research/p1/results/`; tables below are generated by
`research/p1/tables.py`. Each phase reproduces identically with `--reproduce`. Every number is a **Coinbase**
historical result, using Coinbase prices, the point-in-time Coinbase universe and **assumed, unverified**
Coinbase costs. None of it is evidence about any other venue. Paper only; nothing here places orders.

## Verdict

**P1 failed on both horizons: no qualifying candidate.**
- **Daily (P1a):** every configuration lost money after costs in development. The candidate (M1, ridge)
  was chosen only because it lost the least. It then lost 0.21% a day in validation, which disqualified it.
- **Weekly (P1b):** no model beat the momentum baseline in development, so there was never a candidate.

The final historical period was opened once, for the record, and no configuration can pass. P1a and P1b are
recorded as REJECTED in `tradelab/governance/status.py`. Nothing is frozen for trading, and no prospective
trial starts.

## What the numbers say

1. **The ranking signals are far too weak for these costs.**
   - The development stopping check passed, but it set a high bar. At the base Coinbase cost, a daily top-3
     selection needs a rank correlation of about 0.10 with the next day's returns, even at momentum's slow
     turnover. At full replacement it needs 0.32.
   - The observed daily rank ICs ranged from −0.04 to +0.06 across all configurations and phases.
2. **Costs dominate.**
   - Short-term reversal (B2) had a statistically positive rank IC in development and in validation
     (+0.035 in both). It churned 70–80% of the portfolio a day, though, and paid about 0.9–1.0% a day in costs.
   - Seven-day momentum (B1) had a *negative* daily rank IC in all three phases.
3. **Abstention worked only as staying out.**
   - The return models (M1, M3) required a predicted return above the 1.32% round trip before entering.
     They stayed in cash for 84–94% of their slots.
   - At the daily horizon, abstaining beat the same model forced to fill all 3 slots in every phase, with
     the interval above zero each time.
   - It did not beat cash. The useful decision the models learned is "don't trade daily at these costs",
     not "these coins will win".
   - At the weekly horizon, abstention's value could not be distinguished from zero.
4. **Picking 3 coins did not add value; it added concentration.**
   - Alpha against the equal-weight universe was negative, or its interval spanned zero, for every
     configuration in every phase.
   - The fully invested daily configurations (B1, B2, M2) had betas of about 1 or more, with drawdowns of 87–100%.
5. **There are a few interesting-looking numbers. None of them is evidence**, because the pre-registered
   process did not select them:
   - M2 (logistic, target-before-stop) was mildly calibrated: Brier skill +0.5% to +1.4%, ECE 0.02–0.03. Its
     rank IC was +0.057 [+0.027, +0.085] in the final period, but only +0.010 in development, and it lost
     heavily traded without abstention.
   - M3 had a positive final Sharpe (0.24, 3/3 thirds positive), but it held positions only 7% of the time,
     its interval includes zero, and it was not the selected candidate.
   - Promoting either now would be choosing after seeing the final period. That period is now spent. Either
     could only be re-tested as a new pre-registered hypothesis on prospective data.
6. **The passive comparisons are sobering.**
   - The equal-weight basket of eligible coins returned −58% in development, −20% in validation and −53% in
     the final period.
   - BTC buy-and-hold returned +44%, +69% and −26% over the same periods.
   - This describes those periods. It is not a strategy, and it is not a reason to hold BTC.
7. **The development ranking of configurations would not have been reliable anyway.** PBO was 0.43 for
   the daily configurations and 0.54 for the weekly ones, against the ≤ 0.05 the pre-registration requires.
8. **Weekly results have little power.** There were 122 out-of-fold development weeks, 53 validation weeks
   and 68 final weeks, and their intervals are wide. "No candidate" here means no evidence of skill, not
   evidence of no skill.

## What P1 rules out, and what it does not

**Ruled out, at Coinbase's assumed retail taker costs:** long-only daily or weekly top-3 selection among liquid
Coinbase coins using price, volume, volatility, trend, relative-strength and breadth features with
regularised linear, logistic or shallow-tree models. Also ruled out at the daily horizon: the simple momentum
and reversal rules.

**Not ruled out:**
- lower execution costs (maker orders, higher fee tiers, another venue measured properly);
- slower policies (monthly, or with a hold buffer);
- market timing (in or out of crypto altogether);
- short selling;
- information other than price and volume (on-chain data, flows, events);
- intraday execution timing;
- stocks, where round-trip costs are about 8 bp rather than 132 bp.

Nor does P1 say the features carry no information. The reversal effect and M2's calibration suggest small
real effects, far too small for these costs. **P1 is not a verdict on AI trading in general.** It is a
verdict on one component, at one cost level, with one data family.

## How P1 bears on the intraday objective

**Transfers to intraday work:**
- the point-in-time universe and bar loaders;
- the causal feature panel, with tests showing a feature never sees later bars;
- labels that enter at the next open and treat missing hours as no-trade;
- folds purged by period, PBO, block-bootstrap inference on per-period portfolio returns, and calibration
  metrics;
- the decision simulator: entry, hold, exit and abstain, with costs on actual turnover and comparators at
  matched risk;
- the gate → development → validation → final-once harness, with deterministic reproduction;
- above all, the **required-skill check**, which tells us before any modelling whether a horizon and cost
  level are feasible at all.

**Needs separate research:**
- intraday features (trades, quotes, order book);
- fill modelling: maker queue position, taker slippage, partial fills;
- measured spreads;
- labels at minute horizons;
- event data;
- latency.

**The main lesson for intraday crypto: cost is the binding constraint.** A strategy that trades more often than
daily needs even more skill per trade at the same cost, and the daily bar is already out of reach. Intraday
crypto is not worth modelling at retail taker fees until execution costs are measured lower. That means
maker fills or a cheaper fee tier, verified on the account. The same check at about 8 bp for liquid stocks
should show a much lower required skill. That is the next thing to compute, and it waits on consolidated (SIP)
quotes.
